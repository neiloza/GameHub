# Animas — Proposed Stat Spreads

Twenty-five stat lines. Every one is a redistribution of the same 100/100/100/100
baseline, and nothing comes close to the 500 ceiling — the spread runs 380 to 410.

---

## How the four stats read

**HP** — the stat is an index, not the hit points. **Hit points = HP stat × 2.5**,
so a stat of 100 is the 250-hit-point baseline the damage formula is built
around. A stat of 140 is 350 hit points; a stat of 80 is 200. Every stat is a
multiple of ten, so every hit-point total lands on a multiple of twenty-five.

**Power** — the damage multiplier, divided by 100. A Power of 120 multiplies
every move's printed number by 1.2. A Power of 70 multiplies it by 0.7.

**Speed** — turn order, high acting first. Nothing else.

**Mana** — the pool, and **the hundred is a wall.** A character at 100 or above
can use its type's signature move. A character below cannot, ever. Ten of the
twenty-five are above the line.

## The three rules behind these numbers

**One. Stat total is the counterweight to Ability strength.** Kelpie has the most
oppressive Ability in the game — the opponent simply cannot leave — so it has the
lowest total on the roster at 380. Loch Ness, Hydra and Roc all sit at 390 for
the same reason. The characters at 410 are the ones whose Abilities need a
setup, a condition, or a sacrifice before they do anything.

**Two. The core nine average slightly higher than the duals**, 405 against 393.
That is the compensation for mono-typing. A single type means half the coverage
and half the movepool, and it has to be paid for somewhere.

**Three. Nothing is good at everything.** Every character that hits above 110
Power is short somewhere obvious: Garuda and Roc cannot afford their signatures,
Gugalanna cannot move, Hydra cannot move, Phoenix folds to anything that connects.

## THE CORE NINE

| Character | HP | Power | Speed | Mana | Total | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fafnir** 🔥 | **140** | 90 | **60** | 110 | 400 | ✅ Pyroclasm |
| **Kelpie** 💧 | 90 | 80 | **130** | **80** | **380** | ❌ |
| **Leshy** 🌿 | **150** | 80 | **50** | **130** | 410 | ✅ Bloom |
| **Raiju** ⚡ | **80** | 90 | **140** | 100 | 410 | ✅ Overload |
| **Garuda** 🌪️ | 90 | **120** | **140** | **60** | 410 | ❌ |
| **Sun Wukong** 👊 | 90 | 100 | **130** | 90 | 410 | ❌ |
| **Baku** 🔮 | **130** | **70** | **60** | **150** | 410 | ✅ Mind Crush |
| **Grim Reaper** 🌑 | 110 | 90 | 120 | 90 | 410 | ⚠️ *see below* |
| **Unicorn** ✨ | 110 | **70** | 120 | 110 | 410 | ✅ Resurrection |

**Fafnir** is built to survive its own Ability. Slow Burn needs three turns in
play before Power doubles, so the dragon gets 350 hit points and the second-worst
Speed in the game to pay for it. Ninety Power doubling to 180 on turn four is the
single largest swing on the roster, and it is telegraphed for three turns first.

**Kelpie has 80 Mana, which is the most important number on this page.** Drown
costs 100. **Kelpie cannot use Drown.** The Bind-and-Drown lock I have flagged
since the move list was written does not exist — the character that traps you for
free is not the character that can kill you for it. It has to hold you in place
while a teammate does the work, which is a far better card than an unanswerable
two-move combination.

**Leshy** is the wall the type deserves: 375 hit points, 130 Mana for Bloom, and a
Speed of 50 that means it acts last against almost everything. It wins by not
dying.

**Raiju** is the fastest character in the game at 140 with only 200 hit points to
show for it. Battery restores 25 Mana a turn against a 100 pool, so Raiju refills
in four turns where everyone else takes ten. Note that Overload costs "your whole
bar" rather than a flat hundred, so Raiju is comfortably able to use it.

**Garuda** is a striker, not a controller. 120 Power and 140 Speed, and 60 Mana
means Tempest is out of reach forever. It hits first and hard and cannot erase
the board.

**Sun Wukong** at 90 Mana cannot cast Frenzy, which is correct — Bloodlust is the
win condition, and stacking 50% Power per knockout is what the character is for.

**Baku** has the highest Mana in the game at 150 and the worst offensive stats of
any core member. It is a lock, not a fighter. Pressure doubles the opponent's
costs, and 150 Mana means Baku can still cast Mind Crush while its opponent
cannot cast anything.

**The Grim Reaper is the roster's one exception and it is deliberate.** At 90 Mana
it sits below the wall — but Harvest halves Death Touch to 50, so the Reaper is
**the only character in the game that fires a signature move from under the
breakpoint.** One cast immediately, a second about five turns later. That is a
better Ability than "cheaper instant kill" and it is the whole reason the
character exists.

**Unicorn** is 70 Power and 110 Mana: a support that happens to be unkillable
once. Miracle only triggers from full health, which rewards keeping it on the
bench and bringing it in clean.

**Phoenix** is the frailest thing on the roster at 200 hit points, and it does not
matter, because Rebirth gives it a second one. 110 Power and 130 Speed for two
short lives instead of one long one.

**Xiuhcoatl** has 70 Mana on purpose. Cometfall deals 50 damage on every switch-in
and repeats forever, so the serpent is built to pivot rather than to cast. Low
Mana is what stops it doing both.

**Bakeneko** at 140 Speed is tied for fastest, which is what a Metronome character
wants — whatever it rolls, it rolls first.

**Hydra** is a slow, heavy pivot: 325 hit points, 110 Power, and 70 Speed, with
Regenerator returning half its health every time it leaves. It is designed to be
switched, not to outrun anything.

**Matsya** is 70 Power and 130 Mana — pure support. Forewarning reveals the
opponent, and the Mana is there for Renewal and Resurrection.

**World Turtle holds four records: highest HP at 425 hit points, lowest Speed at
30, joint-lowest Power at 70.** It is the world resting on a shell. It is also
the best Inversion partner in the game by a mile — under a reversed turn order,
the slowest character on the board acts first, every turn.

**Airavata** is Indra's white elephant: 375 hit points, 50 Speed, and 110 Mana.
First Answer doubles each move the first time it is used, so four doubled moves
from a 90-Power body, delivered slowly. It is the definition of a setup character
that needed HP to survive setting up.

**Roc carries elephants and has 50 Mana to prove it.** 120 Power on a 300-hit-point
frame, and the lowest Mana in the game — Roc has almost no access to its own
type's tools. It walks in, Intimidates, and hits things.

**Kitsune** is 200 hit points and 130 Speed, sitting exactly on the Mana wall at
100. Illusion breaks the moment anything connects, so the fox is built never to
be hit.

**Fenrir** at 100 Power doubles to 200 under Berserk, and 70 Mana means it will
never cast Death Touch or Frenzy. Berserk *is* its signature, and the 275
hit points give it a real window below half health to use it.

**Gugalanna** drank a river dry and opened pits that swallowed hundreds of men:
350 hit points, 120 Power, and a Speed of 60. It is the second-slowest heavy
hitter in the game and it does not care, because Seven Years of Famine works
whether the bull acts or not.

## What the spread looks like across the roster

| Stat | Lowest | Highest |
|---|---|---|
| **HP** | Raiju, Phoenix, Kitsune — 80 *(200 hit points)* | World Turtle — 170 *(425)* |
| **Power** | Baku, Unicorn, Matsya, World Turtle — 70 | Garuda, Roc, Gugalanna — 120 |
| **Speed** | World Turtle — 30 | Raiju, Garuda, Bakeneko — 140 |
| **Mana** | Roc — 50 | Baku — 150 |

**Ten of twenty-five can use a signature move.** That is the distribution that
makes the hundred-mana wall worth having — common enough to matter, rare enough
that a stat line answers the question at a glance.

**Every high-Power character pays for it somewhere obvious.** Garuda has 60 Mana.
Roc has 50. Gugalanna has 60 Speed. Hydra has 70 Speed. Phoenix has 200 hit
points. There is no character on this roster that is fast, strong, tough and
loaded at once, and the 500 cap is never the thing preventing it — the design is.

## Open items

1. **Does Power scale healing and percentage effects, or only damage?** It should
   only touch damage, or a 120-Power Light character heals for more than a
   70-Power one and the support types get worse for being supportive.
2. **Speed ties.** Two characters at 130 need a resolution rule, and it cannot be
   random. The cleanest options are that the defender acts first, or that the
   character who switched in more recently acts last.
3. **Are the core nine's extra ten points enough?** 405 against 393 is a thin
   compensation for losing half your coverage. It may need to be 420 against 390
   once the types are played against each other.
4. **Kelpie at 80 Mana is the single most load-bearing number here.** It is what
   dismantles the Bind-and-Drown lock. If Kelpie is ever raised to 100, that
   combination comes straight back.
